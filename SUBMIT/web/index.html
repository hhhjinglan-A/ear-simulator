Start here

  1. Look at the black curve. It is the combined outer + middle ear response. The peak near 3 kHz is what makes speech frequencies loudest at the cochlea.
  2. Move one of the three highlighted sliders on the left. They are the three parameters the assignment asks you to experiment with — an inertance, a compliance and a loss. Everything else is folded into Advanced; you do not need it to start.
  3. Press "Set baseline" first, then move a slider. The grey dashed curve stays where you were, so you can see exactly what your change did.
  4. When you can predict what a slider will do before you move it, go to the Experiments tab and record it properly.

How to read the three plots: magnitude is how much louder or quieter each frequency gets; phase is how much each frequency is delayed; input impedance is how hard the middle ear is to push — it is what a clinician measures with a tympanometer, not something you hear.

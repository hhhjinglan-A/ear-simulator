Reading the plots. Magnitude is pressure gain: how much bigger the sound pressure at the cochlea is than at the input, in dB. It is not loudness — loudness is a perceptual quantity that also depends on level and on the listener. Phase is the angle by which each frequency component is shifted, in degrees. A phase shift is not a time delay. Two different delays can be derived from it: phase delay −φ/ω and group delay −dφ/dω, both with φ in radians and ω = 2πf in rad/s. Group delay is the one that describes when an envelope arrives. Note that it is the derivative with respect to angular frequency on a linear scale, so the visual steepness of this curve — degrees against a logarithmic frequency axis — is not the group delay and cannot be read off by eye; a constant delay would look curved here, not straight. Input impedance is how hard the middle ear is to drive; it is what a tympanometer measures, and it is not something you hear.
